## Recurring PARCS reasoning patterns

### 1. Role boundaries & residual responsibility

Misses: **3.2 Q4, 4.1 Q1, 9.1 Q5, 10.3 Q2**.

PARCS repeatedly tests the exact role the architect is performing at the relevant moment. A separate specialist, contractor duty, client instruction or certifier function does not automatically erase the architect’s own obligations. Conversely, an architect does not automatically become liable for work that belongs to another party merely because the architect administers the contract.

Key distinctions:
- non-cash remuneration can create a direct financial/proprietary conflict;
- specialist consultant liability can coexist with architect principal-consultant/coordination liability;
- fact-finding undertaken specifically for certification can be part of the architect’s independent assessor role rather than an owner-agent role;
- a contractor workmanship obligation is different from rectification of an architect design/detailing omission.

### 2. Change control: assess against the current documented position

Misses: **5.2 Q3 and 5.2 Q4**.

A change is measured against the current agreed/documented position, not against the project’s historical starting point. Reinstating an earlier design can still involve additional work because the architect has already acted on an intervening instruction. Deal with authority, fee and program consequences before simply implementing the next change.

### 3. Preserve the tender basis before changing it

Misses: **7.1 Q1 and 7.2 Q1**.

PARCS favours preserving a fair common tender basis until a formal change is actually necessary. Do not prematurely issue an addendum or redesign because a supply risk might eventuate. When a tender is non-conforming, clarify whether the tenderer will stand by its price on the conforming basis before adopting the substitution or creating a new post-close competition.

### 4. Urgent action does not automatically justify abandoning the project chain

Miss: **8.2 Q4**.

Where damage is continuing and the head contractor cannot be contacted, prompt mitigation is required. PARCS nevertheless distinguishes contacting the existing project subcontractor from independently engaging an unrelated third party. Preserve existing contractual relationships as far as the emergency reasonably permits.

### 5. Separate delay entitlement from overlap, causation and cost

Miss: **10.1 Q2**.

First identify whether the event itself is a qualifying owner-risk or contractor-risk delay. Then assess overlap/concurrency, critical path and cost consequences. A separate overlapping delay does not necessarily remove the underlying entitlement to time.

### 6. Defect type and remedy are separate questions

Miss: **11.1 Q3**, with a related distinction in **10.3 Q2**.

Identify whether the problem is workmanship, design, latent defect, post-PC defect or another contractual issue, then identify the remedy available at that stage. Preserve the official PARCS answer for 11.1 Q3 exactly: **have the defects rectified and then issue a claim to the architect for the rectification costs**. This is a course-specific answer and is not to be generalised as a universal replacement for the applicable defect/security procedure under every contract.

## Adaptive-learning implementation

The app now:

- marks the 11 missed answer points as **PARCS calibration traps**;
- groups them into six recurring reasoning patterns;
- prioritises unseen calibration-trap questions ahead of ordinary unseen questions within an adaptive session;
- gives trap questions a modest review-priority boost after they have been seen;
- provides dedicated **PARCS calibration trap** drills in the Stats / Focus Areas screen;
- starts each calibration drill with the exact official trap question and then mixes related source-verified questions;
- displays the calibration lesson only after the answer is revealed, so it does not cue the answer in advance;
- keeps personal weak-area detection separate, so calibration traps are not falsely labelled as the user’s own weak areas.
