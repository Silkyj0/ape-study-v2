# Modules 5–11 quality assurance audit

Date: 7 September 2026

## Scope and standard

This audit reviewed the live authored and PARCS-confirmed question sets for Modules 5–11 against four criteria:

1. **Source fidelity and answer defensibility** — does the keyed answer reflect the supplied PARC/Acumen reading and avoid silently importing unsupported rules?
2. **Exam difficulty** — does the item require judgement, role identification, sequencing or application rather than merely recognising a definition?
3. **Distractor quality and ambiguity** — are the alternatives plausible enough to test understanding without creating two defensible answers?
4. **Explanation quality** — does the post-answer material explain the controlling distinction, not merely restate the correct option?

PARCS-confirmed questions remain immutable. Their wording and official answer keys were not changed, including course-specific answers that are deliberately retained as calibration points.

## Overall finding

The substantive answer bank for Modules 5–11 is strong. No new authored keyed-answer conflict was identified in this audit. The main weakness was **uneven difficulty**, not source accuracy: many authored questions in Modules 6, 7 and 11 were clear and correct but too close to one-step recall compared with the official PARCS samples. Modules 8–10 already contained more decision-based contract-administration material.

A targeted set of **33 authored questions** has therefore been upgraded without changing the source proposition or stored correct-answer position. The upgrades add project facts, competing duties and plausible distractors so the learner has to identify the controlling rule rather than recognise a familiar phrase. The upgraded questions also carry an optional **Exam reasoning** note shown only after answering.

## Source fidelity checks performed during this audit

### Tendering

The authored tender questions remain consistent with the supplied receiving/selecting reading: material tender clarifications should preserve a common basis; insufficient tenders may be extended or re-called before opening; a suspected erroneous tender should be confirmed or withdrawn rather than rewritten; substantial scope changes generally require new tenders; negotiation may begin with the lowest valid tenderer after notification; and contract-formation wording in letters of acceptance requires care.

### Inspections and opening up

The inspection questions remain consistent with the supplied reading: partial inspection services are high risk and fee saving is not an exceptional justification; architects should prepare for inspections and keep records; standard contracts commonly provide a power to order opening up; and compliant opened-up work can create owner cost consequences. The audit therefore strengthened the judgement context without changing the propositions.

### Defects and post-completion liability

The defects questions remain consistent with the supplied reading: contractor responsibility generally concerns defective workmanship/material failure within its responsibility; poor design/detailing is not simply a contractor defect; latent defects require that reasonable inspection could not have revealed them; architects should avoid liability admissions and notify PI insurers where appropriate; and contractual notice/rectification procedures matter before an owner uses another person to rectify contractor defects.

### Final completion

The Module 11 authored treatment remains consistent with the supplied final-completion reading: final completion is typically after the defects-liability period, remaining retention is commonly released then, and an ABIC final certificate is **evidence** rather than an absolute bar to later claims, subject to recognised exceptions including latent defects.

## Explanation standard after audit

Not every straightforward factual question needs a long explanation. Over-explaining a simple definition can make study slower without improving judgement. The adopted standard is:

- **simple factual item:** concise explanation identifying the source rule;
- **applied authored item:** explanation identifies the controlling distinction and why the tempting alternative fails;
- **high-value upgraded item:** explanation plus a short **Exam reasoning** note that states the reusable decision rule;
- **PARCS calibration trap:** preserve the official answer and separately display the confirmed calibration lesson.

This provides more depth where it improves exam transfer without turning every answer into a mini-textbook.

## Remaining limitations / deliberate choices

- The official PARCS questions contain some awkward grammar and some unusually blunt answer choices. These are preserved because they are supplied course material.
- The Module 11 Scenario 11.1 Q3 official answer remains a specific PARCS calibration point and must not be silently rewritten to match a broader interpretation of ordinary ABIC defect/security procedure.
- Module 5 remains intentionally compact because the user previously chose not to expand PARC-linked external websites into a large authored bank.
- Difficulty is raised through **application and plausible competing duties**, not through obscure trivia. The goal is to approximate PARCS judgement questions, not make questions artificially difficult.

## QA conclusion

Modules 5–11 are suitable for continued study and for the 40-question exam simulator. The source and answer layer is materially sound; the targeted difficulty pass corrects the principal weakness identified in this audit. Future changes should prioritise learner performance data and user flags rather than further broad question-volume expansion.
